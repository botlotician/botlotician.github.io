Botlotician Promise Tracker

The President's 2024 promises, and what candidates on your 2026 ballot are promising

A nonpartisan tracker for the whole country. It follows the status of the current president's 2024 campaign promises, and lists what candidates on the Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026 ballot say they will do, with a link to the source for every promise. Enter any U.S. ZIP code to see your U.S. House district, your state's U.S. Senate and governor races, and the candidates running. Each race is marked Promises documented (we have sourced promises) or Promises not documented yet, with a link to your state's official election office. Promises are documented so far for dozens of U.S. Senate and governor races across the country, plus California statewide races, U.S. House CA-18, Assembly District 29 and Hollister / San Benito County local races. The site records promises as stated and does not endorse any candidate or judge whether a plan is feasible.

Methodology & caveats

2026 races and candidates

There are no separate state or local tabs. Every 2026 race appears in the ZIP lookup, and each race says who votes in it.

  • Promises documented vs. not documented yet: "Promises documented" means we have collected sourced promises for the candidates in that race. "Promises not documented yet" means we list the race and, where Ballotpedia has them, the general-election candidates, with links to the race and to the state's official election office, but no promises yet. Documented so far: many U.S. Senate and governor races nationwide, California statewide offices, Board of Equalization District 2, U.S. House CA-18, Assembly District 29, and Hollister / San Benito County local races.
  • Which races are listed: every 2026 U.S. Senate race (33 regular elections plus special elections in Florida and Ohio), the 36 governor's races, the D.C. mayor's race, and all 435 U.S. House seats plus D.C.'s non-voting delegate. The race list and candidate names come from Ballotpedia's Senate, gubernatorial and state-by-state U.S. House pages (retrieved Oct. 21, 2026). Write-in candidates are not listed. Candidate lists can change, so check your sample ballot.
  • Louisiana: on Nov. 3, Louisiana holds a nonpartisan primary for most U.S. House seats under its new map. If no candidate wins a majority, the top two meet on Dec. 12, 2026. Those races are labeled.
  • State legislature, local races and ballot measures outside California are not tracked yet. Use the sample ballot links in your ZIP results.
  • What counts as a promise: a specific, forward-looking commitment the candidate made in their own words. Promises are paraphrased or quoted from the linked source. Candidates are listed alphabetically within each race. Party labels are as listed on the ballot. Local offices are nonpartisan.
  • Sources: candidate campaign websites, the California Official Voter Information Guide statements, candidate questionnaires (e.g., GrowSF), and news coverage and Q&As (LA Times, Sacramento Bee, KCRA, ABC7, Daily News, BenitoLink, the Free Lance). Every promise links to its source.
  • California ballot confirmation: statewide and legislative contests come from the CA Secretary of State's Certified List of Candidates (Aug. 27, 2026). Board of Equalization District 2 is confirmed by the SOS BOE district map. Hollister contests are confirmed by the City Clerk elections page. County, school and special-district contests come from Ballotpedia and BenitoLink. The San Benito County elections website returned "Access Denied" to automated requests, so local contests could not be checked against the county's own list.
  • Not on the 2026 ballot in California: California has no U.S. Senate race in 2026. State Senate District 17 (Hollister) is not up this year, because only even-numbered Senate districts are.
  • Voter's Edge (votersedge.org) has shut down and was not used as a source.

ZIP code lookup

  • ZIP codes are approximate. The lookup uses the Census Bureau's 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs), which closely match but are not identical to USPS ZIP codes. One ZIP can cross city, county, district and even state lines. When it does, every overlapping district is listed with its share of the ZIP's 2020 population. Your exact races depend on your street address, so always check your official sample ballot.
  • U.S. House districts are the ones used on Nov. 3, 2026. They come from the Census Bureau's 120th Congress block equivalency files (the districts for the 2026 elections). Per the Census Bureau, nine states drew new maps for 2026: Alabama, California, Florida, Louisiana, North Carolina, Ohio, Tennessee, Texas and Utah. We compared the 120th and 119th Congress files block by block, and exactly those nine states changed. Missouri's 2025 map is not used: it was blocked before the election, and the Census file uses the 2022 map. California's map (Prop. 50 / AB 604) was also checked block by block against the AB 604 equivalency file.
  • At-large and D.C.: Alaska, Delaware, North Dakota, South Dakota, Vermont and Wyoming each have one at-large House seat. D.C. elects a non-voting delegate and has no U.S. Senators or governor (its mayor is on the 2026 ballot). ZIPs in U.S. territories and military (APO/FPO) ZIPs get a message instead of races.
  • California districts: State Assembly and State Senate come from the Census Bureau's 2026 state legislative block equivalency files (the 2021 Citizens Redistricting Commission maps). Board of Equalization: the Commission's final 2021 BOE equivalency file. Only even-numbered State Senate districts are on the 2026 ballot.
  • Places and population: Census 2020 ZCTA-to-block relationship file and 2020 P.L. 94-171 block populations, county and place codes. "Inside city/town limits" means inside an incorporated place. Unincorporated communities (CDPs) are listed when they make up part of a ZIP. ZIP data built Oct. 21, 2026.
  • Official links: each state's election office comes from USA.gov's list of state election offices. California county offices come from the Secretary of State's county list.
  • PO Box ZIPs have no Census area. 95024 (Hollister PO Boxes) is shown with the districts of 95023, the Hollister ZIP it sits inside. Other PO Box or business ZIPs show their state's Senate and governor races only.

    President's 2024 promises

    • Selection: 46 of the most notable, specific promises, drawn mainly from PolitiFact's 75-promise MAGA-Meter, the 2024 Republican platform, Agenda47 videos, and pledges from rallies, interviews and debates.
    • Ratings are PolitiFact-based: Promise Kept → Kept; Compromise → Partially kept; In the Works → In progress; Stalled → Stalled; Broken → Broken. Some notes add newer reporting from the Congressional Research Service, Reuters and CNN. Statuses are as of Oct. 21, 2026.
    • Ratings can be out of date: each one reflects PolitiFact's latest update for that promise, and some were last updated in 2025. Statuses may have changed since then.
    • Dates: some pledges were first made in 2023 and repeated in 2024. For a few promises, the date shown is when PolitiFact listed it (Sept. 30, 2024), not when it was first said. A few dates are approximate.
    • Further context: PBS NewsHour, Feb. 23, 2026; CRS on tariffs, Oct. 2026.
